5 Evidence-Based Ways to Cope with Exam Anxiety

Exam stress cannot simply be solved by "studying less and relaxing more." Cognitive science and psychology research have identified concrete strategies that both reduce anxiety and improve performance.

What is exam anxiety?

Exam anxiety is a state of intense fear and worry that arises in assessment situations. A moderate amount of anxiety can actually improve performance (the Yerkes-Dodson law); but when it becomes excessive, it significantly reduces both study efficiency and exam performance.

Approximately 25–40% of university students experience clinically significant exam anxiety. Among international students, this figure can be higher due to language and cultural differences.

Symptoms of exam anxiety

  • • Panic before or during the exam
  • • Feeling your mind goes blank
  • • Difficulty concentrating
  • • Procrastination
  • • Stomach aches, headaches
  • • Sleep difficulties
  • • Heart palpitations
  • • Feeling inadequate
1

Retrieval Practice

Instead of re-reading material repeatedly, test yourself. Close the book and try to recall. This act of "retrieval" transfers knowledge to long-term memory and reduces exam anxiety — because the brain generates a signal of "I've already practised this."

How to apply: topic summaries, flashcards, explaining to someone else (the Feynman technique)

2

Spaced Repetition

All-night cramming sessions before an exam are both inefficient and increase anxiety. Spacing revision across multiple days increases long-term retention by up to three times. (Ebbinghaus, 1885; updated meta-analyses)

Tool recommendations: Anki, Quizlet or a simple revision timetable

3

Protect sleep quality

Sleep is the process through which newly learned information is consolidated in the brain. Staying up all night before an exam is equivalent to erasing a significant portion of what you learnt during the day. 7–9 hours of sleep both strengthens memory and regulates stress hormones.

Tip: Switch off screens 1 hour before bed; a light revision routine does not disrupt sleep quality.

4

4-7-8 breathing technique

When anxiety intensifies before or during study: inhale through your nose for 4 counts → hold for 7 counts → exhale through your mouth for 8 counts. Repeat this cycle 3–4 times. It activates the parasympathetic nervous system and calms the stress response within minutes.

Can also be applied silently inside the exam hall.

5

Cognitive reframing

Catastrophic thoughts such as "If I fail this exam, my life is over" multiply anxiety. Instead, question the thought: "This exam matters, but whatever the outcome, I can keep going." Stanford research shows that simply re-labelling anxiety as "excitement" improves performance. (Brooks, 2014)

Exercise: Write your worries on paper for 10 minutes before the exam — this "expressive writing" exercise reduces anxiety.

When should you seek professional support?

If exam anxiety is preventing you from sitting exams, causing you to restrict your social life, or leading to intense physical symptoms days in advance, working with a psychologist can fundamentally change the situation. Cognitive Behavioural Therapy has strong evidence for treating exam anxiety.
