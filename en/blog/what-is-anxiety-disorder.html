What is an Anxiety Disorder? Symptoms, Causes and Treatment

Anxiety is a part of life. But when it reaches a level that disrupts daily functioning, we can speak of an "anxiety disorder."

The difference between anxiety and an anxiety disorder

Nervousness before an exam, excitement before an important presentation, or fear when facing a dangerous situation — these are all normal, healthy forms of anxiety. This kind of anxiety motivates and protects us.

An anxiety disorder is different: it is a chronic state of anxiety that arises without a real threat, or with an intensity far disproportionate to any actual threat, and that negatively affects daily life, relationships, and work or academic performance.

Awareness: How common is it?

Approximately 284 million people worldwide live with an anxiety disorder — making anxiety the most prevalent mental health condition globally. (WHO, 2019)

Types of anxiety disorder

Generalised Anxiety Disorder (GAD)

Excessive, uncontrollable worry across multiple areas lasting at least 6 months. Persistent concern about work, health, family, finances and similar topics.

Panic Disorder

Sudden, intense fear episodes (panic attacks) characterised by palpitations, shortness of breath and fear of dying. Consists of recurring panic attacks.

Social Anxiety Disorder

Fear of being judged, humiliated or embarrassed in social situations. Can lead to avoidance of social interactions and isolation.

Specific Phobias

Disproportionate fear of specific objects or situations (heights, enclosed spaces, flying, spiders, etc.).

Symptoms of anxiety disorder

Symptoms can be grouped as emotional, cognitive and physical:

Emotional

  • • Persistent sense of dread
  • • Irritability and tension
  • • Fear of losing control
  • • Panic and terror

Cognitive

  • • Difficulty concentrating
  • • Catastrophic thinking
  • • Indecisiveness
  • • Mind going "blank"

Physical

  • • Palpitations, sweating
  • • Muscle tension
  • • Sleep problems
  • • Shortness of breath

Treatment options

Anxiety disorders are largely manageable with appropriate treatment. The main evidence-based approaches are:

  • Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT)

    Focuses on recognising and changing the thought patterns that fuel anxiety. CBT has the strongest evidence base for anxiety disorders.

  • EMDR

    Particularly effective for anxiety rooted in traumatic experiences.

  • Mindfulness-Based Approaches

    Breaks the anxiety cycle by learning to stay present in the moment.

When should you seek help?

If your anxiety is negatively affecting your work, relationships or daily functioning; if you have developed avoidance behaviours; or if you are experiencing sleep difficulties, it is advisable to speak to a professional.
