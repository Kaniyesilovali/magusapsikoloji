Frequently asked questions

Answers to the questions you have before starting therapy.

Starting Therapy

The first session is a "getting to know you" meeting. The therapist listens to understand you and your reason for coming; you also get to know the therapist. You don't need to share anything you're not ready to — the pace is entirely yours.

A problem doesn't need to be "big enough." If you feel overwhelmed, unhappy or stuck; if you experience recurring difficulties in relationships; if you notice significant changes in sleep, appetite or motivation — therapy may help. You can also start therapy for personal growth without any crisis.

We work through this together in our free discovery call. The type of issue, your preferred approach and the sense of "fit" with the therapist are all important factors. Tell us your needs and we'll match you with the most suitable therapist.

Sessions & Process

Duration varies by person and issue. Short-term CBT typically runs 8–16 sessions, while deeper psychodynamic work may be longer term. EMDR can produce results in fewer sessions in some cases. After the initial assessment, your therapist will present a personalised plan.

Once a week is generally recommended. This regularity supports therapeutic progress. Fortnightly sessions may be sufficient in some cases. Your therapist will make a recommendation based on your needs.

Therapy is voluntary and you can end it whenever you choose. However, we do recommend discussing this with your therapist. A planned ending consolidates gains and reduces any sense of "unfinished business."

Online Therapy

Research shows online sessions for individual therapy are comparably effective to in-person sessions. The online format is particularly well-suited for CBT and talk therapy. Some methods such as EMDR have also been adapted for online delivery.

A stable internet connection and a device with a camera and microphone are sufficient. It's important to be in a private, confidential environment where nobody can see or hear you. Platform details are shared before your appointment.

Confidentiality & Ethics

Yes. Therapist-client confidentiality is the cornerstone of professional ethics. Except for legal exceptions (such as risk of harm to yourself or others, mandatory reporting of child abuse), everything you share is completely confidential and will not be shared with any third party.
